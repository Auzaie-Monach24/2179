1 Spatial overview

Malaysia's housing prices split sharply along the urban-rural divide. Kuala Lumpur (RM 893K) and Putrajaya (RM 865K) sit in a league of their own, anchoring the premium end of the market. Moving outward, the west-coast corridor, Selangor, Penang, Johor, remains elevated but accessible for higher-income households. Labuan (RM 130K), Terengganu (RM 274K) and Kedah (RM 279K) represent the country's most affordable markets. Use the map as a geographic reference before exploring the labour and market-structure charts below.

2 Housing price and labour context
High prices don't always mean weak jobs. Selangor pairs premium prices (RM 622K) with the lowest unemployment nationally (1.8%), a market backed by strong employment. Sabah goes the other way: modest property costs yet 5.7% unemployment, signalling structural labour weakness independent of housing costs. KL anchors the price axis at RM 893K with a moderate 3.2% unemployment rate. Bubble size reflects transaction volume.
Two states own half the market. Johor (15,520 deals) and Selangor (14,146) together represent over 52% of all 2025 transactions, followed by Perak (5,919) at a distant third. KL records just 3,359 deals despite the highest prices, a high-cost, low-volume premium market. Price-per-sq-ft colour separates affordable high-volume states from premium ones.
3 Market structure
Terrace homes dominate active states. In Johor and Selangor, the two busiest markets, terrace-based homes account for the overwhelming majority of transactions, reflecting the continued pull of landed housing in the urban periphery. High-rise properties gain a stronger share in KL and Penang, where land scarcity pushes density upward.
Three categories cover 97% of the market. Terrace-based homes lead with 42,176 deals (75% of total), high-rise homes follow at 11,047 (20%), and Semi-D/Cluster at 2,521 (4.5%). Bungalows and town houses combined account for under 500 transactions, the 2025 market is essentially a two-type story.

Where do active state–type combinations concentrate?

Scan the heatmap: the hottest cells are Johor–terrace and Selangor–terrace, confirming landed housing as the national engine. High-rise activity is more evenly spread along the west coast, KL, Selangor, and Penang all show notable counts.

Toggle the Tenure filter to reveal ownership patterns: freehold terrace homes cluster in Johor, while leasehold high-rise properties dominate KL and Selangor, a distinction with long-term resale implications.

Key takeaway: Terrace + Johor/Selangor = highest liquidity. High-rise + KL/Penang = deepest freehold pool.
4 Quarterly labour movement

Employment holds steady; unemployment stays contained

Across all states, employed-person counts trend upward through 2025, while unemployment figures remain comparatively flat on the right axis, a broadly positive signal for household income capacity and mortgage serviceability.

Select individual states using the filter. Selangor and Johor show consistently rising employment, reinforcing their dominance in housing activity. Sabah exhibits wider quarterly swings, consistent with its structurally higher unemployment rate seen in the scatterplot.

Key takeaway: Strong employment growth in Selangor and Johor explains why these markets sustain high transaction volumes even as prices rise.

Most states trimmed unemployment across 2025

Each dumbbell connects a state's earliest and latest 2025 quarterly unemployment rate. A leftward shift in the right endpoint signals improvement; rightward means conditions worsened.

States with longer connecting lines experienced the most pronounced year-long changes. Sabah and Kelantan, already the weakest performers on the scatterplot, show the widest spreads, making them the most important states to watch for housing-affordability stress driven by income instability.

Key takeaway: Sabah's persistently high unemployment, despite modest property prices, suggests affordability is constrained by earnings rather than house prices.
5 Advanced market profile and local hotspots

Volume leaders are not price leaders

The aligned profile places transaction bars and median-price dots on a shared state axis so both dimensions can be read at a glance, no angles or radii to misread.

Johor tops the volume bar by a wide margin, yet its median price sits well below Selangor and KL. KL anchors the far right of the price axis despite a thin transaction count, confirming that prestige pricing and market depth follow different drivers.

Key takeaway: Buyers seeking future liquidity should favour Johor or Selangor, where a larger pool of comparable sales reduces valuation risk at exit.

A handful of townships drive each state's totals

Beneath state averages, transactions concentrate heavily in a small number of townships. Removing the top five localities from Johor or Selangor would materially shrink each state's headline count, the long tail is real.

Use the State and Type filters to compare active terrace corridors in Johor against high-rise hotspots in Selangor and KL. Lollipop length makes inter-township differences immediately visible without a cluttered axis.

Key takeaway: Township selection matters as much as state selection, hyperlocal demand drives future price growth, not state-level averages alone.
